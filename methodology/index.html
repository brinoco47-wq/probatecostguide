Methodology: How We Research Every Figure

Last updated October 2026

Not legal advice. This page describes a research process, not legal guidance.

Source hierarchy

  1. State statutes. The controlling law — fee schedules set by statute (e.g. California Probate Code), creditor periods, small-estate thresholds. Always first.
  2. Court fee schedules. Published by each state's court system. Filing fees, copy fees, publication requirements.
  3. Published bar and economic surveys. Attorney hourly-rate and flat-fee data from state bar economics surveys and reputable legal publishers. Used for market ranges, never presented as statutory.
  4. Practitioner guidance. State bar consumer guides and court self-help centers. Used for procedural description, clearly labeled.

What we never do

  • Never copy an unsourced figure from another blog and present it as fact.
  • Never present a single attorney's anecdote as a statewide average.
  • Never publish a number without a check date.

Ranges and labeling

Probate costs vary by county, estate complexity, and professional. We publish planning ranges and label what we know vs. what we estimate: figures traced to a statute or schedule are stated directly; market-derived figures are labeled as typical ranges from practitioner surveys.

Corrections

Statutes and fee schedules change. We re-check core figures on a rolling basis and fix verified errors promptly, noting the correction date on the page. Report an error via the contact page.
